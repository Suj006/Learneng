## 1. Cambridge English: Movers Word List Picture Book (2014)

### What it is

A 32-page picture book for children who have finished Starters (CEFR A1 track, roughly ages 7–10). It has:

- eight double-page illustrated scenes, each with a short scene text, labelled picture words and three or four "Let's talk!" questions;
- word games (numbers, word search, opposites);
- the A–Z Movers word list;
- an answer key.

### Vocabulary coverage

- **352 parsed entries** (349 unique headwords plus a few multi-sense entries).
- Parts of speech: 202 nouns, 60 adjectives, 60 verbs, 42 adverbs, 18 prepositions, plus pronouns, determiners and interrogatives.
- 25 multi-word items, such as *bus station*, *get dressed* and *be called*.
- The list also expects numbers 21–100, ordinals 1st–20th, and character names.

### The eight themes, by educational value

| Theme | Word focus | Method insight |
|---|---|---|
| A favourite toy shop | toys, animals, people (pirate, clown), adjectives (tall, fat, naughty, surprised) | Busy scene, so learners hunt for objects and describe them |
| The party | family (grandparent, grandson), food and table items, actions (cry, hide), quiet | Family relationships in context; feelings |
| At the doctor's | body parts, illnesses (cough, cold, earache, headache), better, nurse | Health language through a story situation |
| Uncle Charlie's hotel | upstairs/downstairs, lift/elevator, towel, blanket, address, call | Places in a building; British/American pair shown |
| From the countryside to the jungle | landscape (river, waterfall, island, field), transport, food | Journey sequence from city to jungle |
| The weather | weather nouns and adjectives (sun/sunny, wind/windy) | Noun → adjective word families; days of the week |
| Our town | places (bank, library, market, supermarket), directions (straight, opposite) | Map and directions language |
| Dreaming of holidays | -ing activities (sailing, fishing, texting), technology | Verb forms and imagination |

### Learning method

The book never teaches words by definition alone. Its method has four parts:

1. One rich scene per topic.
2. Labelled cut-outs that link word to picture.
3. "Let's talk!" questions that move a child from one-word answers to opinions and personal experience.
4. Games for recognition and spelling.

PING VIBE had word cards, quizzes and games, but no scene-based discovery. That gap is closed by the new **Word Scenes** feature (see the blueprint).

### Fit with PING VIBE's grades

The Movers level fits **Band 2 (Grades 1–2)** and stretches into Grade 3. Concrete scene nouns (cheese, pool, mountain) also suit Band 1 for picture recognition.

- 96 Movers words already exist in PING VIBE.
- 248 are new.
- 34 were excluded:
  - 22 function words;
  - 5 modal verbs;
  - 4 dated technology words;
  - 2 abbreviations;
  - 1 American form.

## 2. Cambridge English: Preliminary and Preliminary for Schools Vocabulary List (2012)

### What it is

The B1 (CEFR) vocabulary guide for item writers. It has:

- an alphabetical list (pages 5–43);
- Appendix 1, word sets (numbers, days, months, seasons, countries and nationalities, continents);
- Appendix 2, topic lists (pages 40–50, 23 topics).

The list covers both receptive and productive vocabulary but does not mark which is which. It uses examples only to restrict a word's meaning, for example *heel* means shoes only. It lists multi-word verbs only when their meaning is not literal.

### Vocabulary coverage

- **2,971 parsed entries** (2,949 unique keys).
- Parts of speech: 1,729 nouns, 537 adjectives, 528 verbs, 206 adverbs.
- 87 phrasal verbs (*find out*, *give in*, *look forward to*) and 274 multi-word entries.
- 427 entries carry sense-restricting notes. These are flagged as **multi-meaning: create separate sense records, never merge**.
- 17 explicit British/American pairs, such as autumn/fall and lift/elevator.
- Topic coverage is strongest in Travel and Transport (158), Food and Drink (143), Personal Feelings (133), House and Home (131) and Work and Jobs (110). It is weakest in Colours, Services and Language.
  - The Environment block could not be parsed reliably, so it is recorded as a gap in the extraction rather than in the source.

### Suitability for children: B1 is not "Grade 7"

The list was written for teenage and adult exam candidates, so suitability was judged word by word:

- **Age screen:** 39 entries were set aside for review, covering crime, alcohol, relationship break-ups, death and similar topics. The exam excludes war and politics, but not these.
- **Function words:** 159 grammar words and 10 modal verbs belong in Grammar Galaxy, not on vocabulary cards.
- **Dated technology:** 14 words such as *answerphone*, *fax* and *CD-ROM* are low value today.
- **American forms:** 9 entries. British/Indian English stays the primary form and the American form is kept as a variant.
- **Banding.** Each remaining entry was banded from corpus frequency (wordfreq Zipf scale), concreteness (membership of a concrete topic list) and syllable count:
  - Band 2: 1,120 (very common or concrete everyday words)
  - Band 3: 800
  - Band 4: 736, of which 214 are **Challenge** and receptive
  - Band 1: 66
- **Receptive vs productive (PING VIBE's own classification):** 2,142 productive (frequent or concrete) and 580 receptive (recognise in reading first).

## 3. Gaps in existing PING VIBE vocabulary

The audit covered 863 unique records: 120 published, plus 757 queued (14 of the queued words are already published, leaving 743 unique).

- 96 PING VIBE words also appear in Movers and 461 in Preliminary.
- 86 existing words sit two or more bands away from the Cambridge-based suggestion. They are listed for **review only**; nothing was moved automatically.
- 196 existing words have no category. Categories were inferred from Cambridge topics or queue tags, so these must be categorised in the CMS.

### Category coverage against the 30-category taxonomy

| Coverage | Categories |
|---|---|
| Critical gaps (fewer than 10 words) | School and Education, Animals, Transport, Colours and Shapes, Time, Places, Community Helpers, Technology, Science, Geography, Environment, Music, Relationships, Time and Frequency, Money and Shopping, Values and Character |
| Gaps (10–24) | Body and Health, Weather and Seasons, Sports and Hobbies, Clothing, Arts and Culture, Communication |
| Adequate or strong | Actions (148), Descriptions (114), Thinking and Problem-solving (83), Nature (52), Emotions (51), Home and Family, Food, Travel |

The existing bank is rich in verbs and adjectives but thin in topic nouns, which are exactly what scene-based learning needs.

The Cambridge lists help fill Community Helpers, Technology, Arts and Culture, Places, Body and Health and Money and Shopping. They do not cover Science, Geography, Environment, Music, Values or Time and Frequency well. **Those need PING VIBE-authored word sets.**

## 4. Recommendations

1. **Wave 1, ready for review:** enrich and approve the 520 recommended candidates.
    - Band split: Band 1: 40, Band 2: 180, Band 3: 150, Band 4: 150.
    - They are already in the Content Studio queue with full provenance.
2. **Senses:** create separate sense records for the 427 multi-meaning entries as they are enriched (for example *light* as a noun and as an adjective).
3. **Phrasal verbs:** teach the 87 phrasal verbs in Band 4 through context (stories, Real Life scenarios). Link each to its base verb as a word family.
4. **Author new sets** for Science, Geography, Environment, Music, Values and Time and Frequency, which the references do not cover.
5. **Scene-first teaching:** build more Word Scenes as content grows (school, kitchen, sports day, festival).
6. **Review the 86 band mismatches** with a teacher. Use learning evidence, not the reference list, to decide.
